In Lesson 2, you learned weak and strong adjective endings for **nominative and accusative** only. Now we extend the system to **dative** as well (Nom, Acc, Dat) and all **three types** (weak, mixed, strong). We also introduce the **mixed** type (after ein-words) for the first time. The genitive case itself — and the genitive row of these same tables — isn't introduced until Lesson 4, once you've learned what the genitive case actually means.

### 4.1. Weak Endings After der-Words — Complete Table

Used after: **der, die, das, dieser, jeder, welcher, alle, manche**

| Case | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter | Plural |
|------|-----------|----------|--------|--------|
| Nom | -**e** | -**e** | -**e** | -**en** |
| Acc | -**en** | -**e** | -**e** | -**en** |
| Dat | -**en** | -**en** | -**en** | -**en** |

**Pattern:** Only **5 slots** get **-e** (Nom masc/fem/neut + Acc fem/neut). Everything else is **-en**. (A genitive row completes this table in Lesson 4.)

Examples:
- Nom: **der** alt**e** Mann, **die** klein**e** Frau, **das** groß**e** Kind, **die** neu**en** Bücher
- Acc: **den** alt**en** Mann, **die** klein**e** Frau, **das** groß**e** Kind, **die** neu**en** Bücher
- Dat: **dem** alt**en** Mann, **der** klein**en** Frau, **dem** groß**en** Kind, **den** neu**en** Bücher**n**

### 4.2. Mixed Endings After ein-Words — Complete Table

Used after: **ein, kein, mein, dein, sein, ihr, unser, euer, Ihr**

| Case | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter | Plural (kein/possessives only) |
|------|-----------|----------|--------|-------------------------------|
| Nom | -**er** | -**e** | -**es** | -**en** |
| Acc | -**en** | -**e** | -**es** | -**en** |
| Dat | -**en** | -**en** | -**en** | -**en** |

**Key difference from weak:** In **Nom masc** (-er), and **Nom/Acc neut** (-es), the adjective carries the **case signal** because **ein** has no ending in these slots (ein __ Mann, ein __ Kind). Everywhere else, the endings match weak. (A genitive row completes this table in Lesson 4.)

> **ein** has no plural form, but **kein** and all possessives (mein, dein, sein...) do — their plural follows the same pattern.

Examples:
- Nom: **ein** alt**er** Mann, **eine** klein**e** Frau, **ein** groß**es** Kind, **keine** neu**en** Bücher
- Acc: **einen** alt**en** Mann, **eine** klein**e** Frau, **ein** groß**es** Kind, **keine** neu**en** Bücher
- Dat: **einem** alt**en** Mann, **einer** klein**en** Frau, **einem** groß**en** Kind, **keinen** neu**en** Bücher**n**

### 4.3. Strong Endings Without Article — Complete Table

Used when there is **no article** before the adjective. The adjective itself must carry the full case signal.

| Case | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter | Plural |
|------|-----------|----------|--------|--------|
| Nom | -**er** | -**e** | -**es** | -**e** |
| Acc | -**en** | -**e** | -**es** | -**e** |
| Dat | -**em** | -**er** | -**em** | -**en** |

> These endings are almost identical to the definite article endings (der/die/das/den/dem/des). (A genitive row — with its **-en** exception for masc/neut — completes this table in Lesson 4.)

Examples:
- Nom: alt**er** Wein, kalt**e** Milch, frisch**es** Brot, grün**e** Äpfel
- Acc: alt**en** Wein, kalt**e** Milch, frisch**es** Brot, grün**e** Äpfel
- Dat: mit alt**em** Wein, mit kalt**er** Milch, mit frisch**em** Brot, mit grün**en** Äpfeln

### 4.4. The Signal Concept Summarized

Every German noun phrase needs a **case signal** — a marker that shows the gender, number, and case. The question is: **who carries it?**

**Decision tree:**

1. Is there a **der-word** (der, dieser, jeder, welcher, alle)?
   - **Yes** → The der-word carries the signal → adjective gets **weak** ending (-e or -en)
2. Is there an **ein-word** (ein, kein, mein, dein, sein, ihr, unser, euer)?
   - **Yes** → Does the ein-word show the signal? (Does it have an ending?)
     - **Yes** (eine**n**, eine**r**, eine**m**, etc.) → adjective gets **weak** ending (-e or -en — see table 4.2)
     - **No** (ein ___, mein ___) → adjective carries the signal → **strong-like** ending (-er for masc, -es for neut)
   - This combined pattern is called **mixed**.
3. Is there **no article** at all?
   - **Yes** → Adjective must carry the full signal → **strong** ending

**Quick check:** Look at what comes before the adjective. If it already shows gender/case clearly → adjective relaxes to -e/-en. If not → adjective steps up with a strong ending.

### 4.5. 10 Worked Examples

**Weak (after der-words)**

**Example 1:** "the old man" in nominative
- der-word present (der) → **weak**
- Nom masc → ending: **-e**
- Result: **der alte Mann**

**Example 2:** "this small child" in accusative
- der-word present (dieses) → **weak**
- Acc neut → ending: **-e**
- Result: **dieses kleine Kind**

**Example 3:** "the new books" in dative
- der-word present (den) → **weak**
- Dat pl → ending: **-en**
- Result: **den neuen Büchern**

**Mixed (after ein-words)**

**Example 4:** "a good man" in nominative
- ein-word present (ein) → **mixed**
- ein has no ending here (ein ___) → adjective carries signal
- Nom masc signal → **-er**
- Result: **ein guter Mann**

**Example 5:** "my new car" in nominative
- ein-word present (mein) → **mixed**
- mein has no ending here (mein ___) → adjective carries signal
- Nom neut signal → **-es**
- Result: **mein neues Auto**

**Example 6:** "a beautiful woman" in accusative
- ein-word present (eine) → **mixed**
- eine shows the signal (-e) → adjective relaxes
- Acc fem → **-e**
- Result: **eine schöne Frau**

**Example 7:** "no cold drinks" in dative
- ein-word present (keinen) → **mixed**
- keinen shows the signal (-en) → adjective relaxes
- Dat pl → **-en**
- Result: **keinen kalten Getränken**

**Strong (no article)**

**Example 8:** "fresh bread" in nominative
- No article → **strong**
- Nom neut signal → **-es**
- Result: **frisches Brot**

**Example 9:** "cold water" in accusative
- No article → **strong**
- Acc neut → **-es**
- Result: **kaltes Wasser**

**Example 10:** "with warm milk" in dative
- No article → **strong**
- Dat fem → **-er**
- Result: **mit warmer Milch**
