Lesson 6 previewed the passive as one of werden's three functions (*Das Haus wird gebaut*). This is the full treatment. The **Vorgangspassiv** (process passive) focuses on the **action itself** — who performs it is often unknown, unimportant, or obvious. German news, instructions, and official texts are full of it, so you must be able to both recognize and build it.

### 3.1. Formation: werden + Partizip II

The passive is built with **werden** (conjugated, in position 2) plus the **Partizip II of the main verb at the end**. werden is conjugated as in Lesson 1 — with its irregular vowel change in the du/er forms.

| Tense | Passive form | Example | English |
|-------|--------------|---------|---------|
| Präsens | **wird + Partizip II** | Das Haus wird gebaut. | The house is being built. |
| Präteritum | **wurde + Partizip II** | Das Haus wurde gebaut. | The house was built. |
| Perfekt | **ist + Partizip II + worden** | Das Haus ist gebaut worden. | The house has been built. |

**Active → passive in three steps:**

1. The **accusative object becomes the new subject**. *Der Bäcker bäckt das Brot.* → subject das Brot.
2. The verb becomes **werden + Partizip II** in the correct tense.
3. The old subject becomes an optional **von-phrase** (§3.5): *Das Brot wird (vom Bäcker) gebacken.*

> **Key limitation:** only **transitive verbs with an accusative object** can be passivized normally. If the object is **dative**, it stays dative and the passive sentence has **no subject**: *Mir wird geholfen.* (I am being helped. — helfen + Dative, Lesson 2) — the dative object Mir moves to position 1, the verb stands in position 2, and that is the whole sentence.

### 3.2. Passive in Präsens

**wird/werden + Partizip II** — something is being done, right now or habitually.

**4 examples:**

1. Das Haus **wird** gebaut. (The house is being built.)
2. Die Briefe **werden** jeden Morgen geöffnet. (The letters are opened every morning.)
3. Das Essen **wird** von der Mutter gekocht. (The food is cooked by the mother.)
4. Deutsch **wird** in vielen Ländern gelernt. (German is learned in many countries.)

> **Note:** the Präsens passive covers both "is being done" and "is done regularly" — context decides. In *Die Briefe werden jeden Morgen geöffnet* the time adverb jeden Morgen signals the habitual reading.

### 3.3. Passive in Präteritum

**wurde/wurden + Partizip II** — the narrative past of the passive, standard in written stories and reports.

**4 examples:**

1. Das Haus **wurde** im Jahr 2019 gebaut. (The house was built in 2019.)
2. Der Dieb **wurde** gestern gefasst. (The thief was caught yesterday.)
3. Die Tür **wurde** um zehn Uhr geschlossen. (The door was closed at ten o'clock.)
4. Der Film **wurde** im Kino gezeigt. (The film was shown in the cinema.)

> **Memory aid:** the Präteritum passive is your third and last function of the werden-family: **wurde** + Partizip II = passive, exactly as Lesson 6 §1.4 predicted.

### 3.4. Passive in Perfekt: ist + Partizip II + worden

The Perfekt passive is **ist/sind + Partizip II + worden**. Note the trap: the participle of werden appears here as **worden**, not **geworden**.

**4 examples:**

1. Das Haus **ist** im Jahr 2019 **gebaut worden**. (The house was built in 2019.)
2. Der Brief **ist** gestern **geschickt worden**. (The letter was sent yesterday.)
3. Die Stadt **ist** im Krieg **zerstört worden**. (The city was destroyed in the war.)
4. Das Auto **ist** repariert **worden**. (The car has been repaired.)

> **Trap (critical):** in the passive Perfekt the participle of werden is **worden** — never ~~geworden~~. **geworden** appears only when werden is the main verb meaning "become": *Er ist Lehrer **geworden**.* (He became a teacher.) Rule of thumb: whenever another Partizip II stands in the sentence, werden becomes worden.

### 3.5. The Agent: von + Dativ

When the doer is mentioned, German names it with **von + Dativ** — people, animals, and organizations that actively perform the action. **von dem** contracts to **vom**.

**4 examples:**

1. Das Haus **wird von dem Bauarbeiter** gebaut. (The house is being built by the construction worker.)
2. Der Brief **wurde von meiner Mutter** geschrieben. (The letter was written by my mother.)
3. Das Lied **ist von einem berühmten Komponisten** geschrieben worden. (The song was written by a famous composer.)
4. Die Tür **wird vom Hausmeister** geöffnet. (The door is opened by the caretaker. — vom = von + dem)

> **von + Dativ answers the question "von wem?"** (by whom?) and names a person or institution: von der Mutter, vom Hausmeister, von der Firma.

### 3.6. Instrument: mit + Dativ / Cause: durch + Akkusativ

Two further prepositions cover the instrument and the cause. **mit + Dativ** names the **tool** used; **durch + Akkusativ** names the **cause** or triggering force — often impersonal.

**mit + Dativ — the instrument (3 examples):**

1. Der Brief **wird mit einem Kugelschreiber** geschrieben. (The letter is written with a ballpoint pen.)
2. Das Bild **wurde mit Ölfarben** gemalt. (The picture was painted with oil paints.)
3. Die Tür **ist mit einem Schlüssel** geöffnet worden. (The door was opened with a key.)

**durch + Akkusativ — the cause (3 examples):**

1. Das Fenster **wurde durch den Wind** geöffnet. (The window was opened by the wind.)
2. Viele Unfälle **werden durch Alkohol** verursacht. (Many accidents are caused by alcohol.)
3. Die Stadt **wurde durch das Erdbeben** zerstört. (The city was destroyed by the earthquake.)

> **Choice guide:** **von + Dativ** = the acting person (von meiner Mutter); **mit + Dativ** = the tool (mit einem Schlüssel); **durch + Akkusativ** = the cause or impersonal force (durch den Wind, durch das Erdbeben). When in doubt about a human doer, use von.

### 3.7. Impersonal Passive with es

**Intransitive verbs** (verbs without an accusative object — arbeiten, tanzen, rauchen, feiern) cannot have a passive subject. German solves this with the **impersonal passive**: the dummy subject **es** fills position 1.

**4 examples with es at position 1:**

1. **Es wird** hier nicht geraucht. (There is no smoking here.)
2. **Es wurde** die ganze Nacht getanzt. (There was dancing all night.)
3. **Es wird** heute viel gearbeitet. (A lot of work is being done today.)
4. **Es wird** am Wochenende gefeiert. (There is a celebration at the weekend.)

**es drops when another element takes position 1** — the V2 rule still holds, the verb moves to position 2 and es disappears:

1. **Hier wird** nicht geraucht. (No smoking here.)
2. **Die ganze Nacht wurde** getanzt. (All night there was dancing.)
3. **Heute wird** viel gearbeitet. (Today a lot of work is being done.)
4. **Am Wochenende wird** gefeiert. (At the weekend there is a celebration.)

> **Note:** es in the impersonal passive is only a placeholder — it disappears whenever any other element claims position 1, and it is dropped in subordinate clauses: *Ich weiß, dass hier nicht geraucht wird.* (I know that there is no smoking here.) The dative-object passive from §3.1 (*Mir wird geholfen*) follows the same model: no subject at all, the dative object simply opens the sentence.
