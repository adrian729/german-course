Until now the Perfekt (Lesson 2) has followed one simple frame: **haben/sein in position 2 + Partizip II at the end**. This week you meet the one big exception — the **double infinitive** (in German: **Ersatzinfinitiv**, "replacement infinitive"). Whenever a modal verb (Lesson 2) appears in the Perfekt together with a main verb infinitive, the modal stays in the **infinitive** instead of becoming a Partizip II. You will hear this construction constantly in conversation, because it is the only way Germans form the past of "could", "wanted to", "had to", and "should have".

### 1.1. The Rule: The Infinitive Replaces the Partizip II

The rule is simple: **a modal verb in the Perfekt with a main verb infinitive does not take the Partizip II (gekonnt, gewollt, gemusst ...) — it stays an infinitive.** The two infinitives stand at the very end of the sentence, and the auxiliary **haben** is conjugated in position 2.

| Präsens | Perfekt (double infinitive) | English |
|---------|----------------------------|---------|
| Er **kann** kommen. | Er **hat** kommen **können**. | He could come. |
| Sie **will** schlafen. | Sie **hat** schlafen **wollen**. | She wanted to sleep. |
| Wir **müssen** arbeiten. | Wir **haben** arbeiten **müssen**. | We had to work. |

> **Trap (critical):** the form *gekonnt* disappears when a main verb infinitive is present. **Er hat kommen können** is right; ~~Er hat kommen gekonnt~~ and ~~Er hat gekonnt kommen~~ are both wrong. The only place you will still see gekonnt is without a second verb: *Er hat es **gekonnt**.* (He was able to do it.) The same applies to all six modals: wollen, müssen, sollen, dürfen, mögen.

### 1.2. Word Order in Main Clauses: 6 Examples, One per Modal

The frame in main clauses is exactly the modal frame you know (Lesson 2), with the tense swapped to Perfekt: **Subject + hat + everything else + Infinitive + Modal Infinitive at the end.** Nothing stands after the two infinitives.

**6 examples — one per modal:**

1. **können:** Er **hat** gestern nicht kommen **können**. (He could not come yesterday.)
2. **wollen:** Sie **hat** den Film sehen **wollen**. (She wanted to see the film.)
3. **müssen:** Wir **haben** lange warten **müssen**. (We had to wait a long time.)
4. **sollen:** Ich **habe** ihn anrufen **sollen**. (I was supposed to call him.)
5. **dürfen:** Du **hast** hier nicht rauchen **dürfen**. (You were not allowed to smoke here.)
6. **mögen:** Sie **hat** immer Schokolade essen **mögen**. (She has always liked eating chocolate.)

> **Auxiliary choice:** verbs that normally build their Perfekt with **sein** (kommen, gehen, fahren — Lesson 2) switch to **haben** when a double infinitive stands at the end: *Er ist gekommen* but *Er **hat** kommen können*. With a double infinitive, the auxiliary is always haben — this is a reliable way to recognize the construction.

### 1.3. Double Infinitive in Subordinate Clauses: An Exception to Verb-Final

Every subordinate clause you have learned so far is verb-final (Lesson 2): *..., dass er gekommen **ist**.* The double infinitive breaks that rule. **The finite auxiliary hat moves BEFORE the double infinitive**, so the clause ends with the two infinitives, not with hat.

**4 examples:**

1. Ich weiß, dass er nicht **hat kommen können**. (I know that he could not come.)
2. Sie sagt, dass sie den Film **hat sehen wollen**. (She says that she wanted to see the film.)
3. Er glaubt, dass wir lange **haben warten müssen**. (He believes that we had to wait a long time.)
4. Wir wissen, dass du das nicht **hast tun dürfen**. (We know that you were not allowed to do that.)

> **Trap (critical):** in subordinate clauses the order is **hat + Infinitive + Modal Infinitive** at the end: *dass er **hat kommen können*** — never ~~dass er kommen können hat~~ and never ~~dass er hat gekonnt kommen~~. Compare the normal Perfekt without a modal: *dass er gekommen **ist*** — the auxiliary comes last only when there is no double infinitive.

### 1.4. Double Infinitive with lassen and Perception Verbs

The same replacement applies to two further verb groups that take an infinitive without zu: **lassen** (to have something done — Lesson 12 treats it fully) and the **perception verbs** sehen, hören, fühlen. In the Perfekt they appear as infinitives, never as Partizip II (~~gelassen~~, ~~gesehen~~).

**3 examples:**

1. Sie **hat** das Auto reparieren **lassen**. (She had the car repaired.)
2. Ich **habe** ihn kommen **sehen**. (I saw him come.)
3. Er **hat** die Kinder lachen **hören**. (He heard the children laugh.)

> **Pattern:** the Perfekt of *Er lässt das Auto reparieren* (he has the car repaired) is *Er **hat** das Auto reparieren **lassen***; of *Ich sehe ihn kommen* (I see him coming) it is *Ich **habe** ihn kommen **sehen***. lassen, sehen, hören, fühlen are called "verbs with a bare infinitive" — and exactly like the modals, they stay infinitives in the Perfekt.
