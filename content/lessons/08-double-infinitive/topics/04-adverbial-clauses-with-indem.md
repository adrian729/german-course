The conjunction **indem** ("by doing ...", "by means of ...") connects a main clause to a subordinate clause that names the **method** by which the main action is achieved. It is the B1 replacement for the A2 pattern "I do X, and in that way Y": *Ich lerne Deutsch, **indem** ich jeden Tag übe.* (I learn German by practicing every day.)

### 4.1. Structure: indem + Subordinate Clause

indem behaves like all subordinating conjunctions (Lesson 5): the clause it introduces is **verb-final** and set off by a comma. The subject of both clauses is normally the **same person** — the method belongs to the actor of the main clause.

**4 examples:**

1. Er spart Geld, **indem** er weniger isst. (He saves money by eating less.)
2. Sie lernt Deutsch, **indem** sie jeden Tag übt. (She learns German by practicing every day.)
3. Man bleibt fit, **indem** man sich bewegt. (One stays fit by exercising.)
4. Ich verbessere mein Deutsch, **indem** ich Podcasts höre. (I improve my German by listening to podcasts.)

> **Same-subject rule:** if the doer differs, German uses **dadurch, dass** instead: *Er hilft mir, **dadurch dass** er früh kommt.* (He helps me by coming early.) With indem both clauses share one subject — that is why the subordinate clause names the *how* of the main action.

### 4.2. indem vs. durch + Nominalized Verb: A Preview of Nominalstil

Formal German prefers the **nominal style**: instead of an indem-clause, it uses **durch + nominalized verb** (das Üben, das Essen). You will convert the other way round in Lesson 11's Nominalstil section — here you learn to recognize the equivalence.

**3 contrastive pairs:**

1. Er spart Geld, **indem** er weniger **isst**. — Er spart Geld **durch** weniger **Essen**. (He saves money by eating less.)
2. Sie lernt Deutsch, **indem** sie täglich **übt**. — Sie lernt Deutsch **durch** tägliches **Üben**. (She learns German through daily practice.)
3. Man verbessert die Stadt, **indem** man mehr Bäume **pflanzt**. — Man verbessert die Stadt **durch** das **Pflanzen** von mehr Bäumen. (One improves the city by planting more trees.)

> **Register rule:** the indem-clause is spoken and everyday; **durch + noun** (often with a genitive or von-phrase) is written and formal — news, letters, academic texts. This is the same verb-to-noun conversion you will meet systematically as Nominalstil in Lesson 11.
