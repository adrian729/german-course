### 3.1. Definition

**Nominalstil** (nominal style) is the habit of expressing actions, processes, and qualities as **nouns** instead of verbs and adjectives. Where spoken German says *Weil die Preise **steigen**, kaufen die Leute weniger*, the nominal style writes *Wegen des **Anstiegs** der Preise nimmt der Konsum ab*. Nominalstil is the signature of German academic prose, official documents, business reports, and serious journalism. It is not a mistake and not optional at B2: you must be able to **read** it fluently and **produce** it in formal writing. The core of the style is a small set of noun-forming suffixes (§3.2), the conversion of verb frames into noun phrases (§3.4), and long genitive chains (§3.5).

> **Note:** the opposite, **Verbalstil** (verbal style), is what you use in conversation and informal writing. A B2 speaker switches between the two depending on the register — that choice is the subject of §3.7.

### 3.2. Suffixes: -ung, -heit, -keit, -schaft, -nis, -tum

German turns verbs and adjectives into nouns with a small set of suffixes. The first four — **-ung, -heit, -keit, -schaft** — are **always feminine**. The last two — **-nis** and **-tum** — have their own gender rules, which are treated separately in §3.3.

**15 examples:**

| Suffix | German | Source | English |
|--------|--------|--------|---------|
| -ung | die **Entwicklung** | entwickeln | development |
| -ung | die **Erklärung** | erklären | explanation |
| -ung | die **Untersuchung** | untersuchen | investigation, study |
| -heit | die **Freiheit** | frei | freedom |
| -heit | die **Gesundheit** | gesund | health |
| -keit | die **Möglichkeit** | möglich | possibility |
| -keit | die **Schwierigkeit** | schwierig | difficulty |
| -schaft | die **Freundschaft** | der Freund | friendship |
| -schaft | die **Wissenschaft** | wissen | science |
| -nis | das **Ergebnis** | ergeben | result |
| -nis | das **Geheimnis** | geheim | secret |
| -nis | die **Kenntnis** | kennen | knowledge |
| -tum | das **Eigentum** | eigen | property |
| -tum | der **Reichtum** | reich | wealth |
| -keit | die **Abhängigkeit** | abhängig | dependence |

> **Note:** every -ung noun comes from a verb and keeps its object as a genitive: *die Entwicklung **der Stadt***, *die Untersuchung **der Ursachen***. Learn the suffix and you can produce the nominal style from any verb you know.

### 3.3. Gender Exceptions: -nis and -tum

The suffixes -ung, -heit, -keit, -schaft are reliably feminine. **-nis** and **-tum** are not: the majority are neuter, but a few important exceptions are feminine or masculine. These exceptions are high-frequency words in formal texts, so memorize them as a set.

**-nis — mostly neuter:**

| German | English | Gender pattern |
|--------|---------|----------------|
| das **Ergebnis** | result | neuter (regular) |
| das **Geheimnis** | secret | neuter (regular) |
| das **Verständnis** | understanding | neuter (regular) |
| das **Ereignis** | event | neuter (regular) |
| das **Bedürfnis** | need | neuter (regular) |

**-nis — feminine exceptions:**

| German | English | Gender pattern |
|--------|---------|----------------|
| die **Kenntnis** | knowledge, acquaintance | feminine exception |
| die **Erlaubnis** | permission | feminine exception |
| die **Erkenntnis** | insight, cognition | feminine exception |
| die **Wildnis** | wilderness | feminine exception |

**-tum — mostly neuter:**

| German | English | Gender pattern |
|--------|---------|----------------|
| das **Eigentum** | property | neuter (regular) |
| das **Christentum** | Christianity | neuter (regular) |
| das **Altertum** | antiquity | neuter (regular) |
| das **Wachstum** | growth | neuter (regular) |

**-tum — masculine exceptions:**

| German | English | Gender pattern |
|--------|---------|----------------|
| der **Reichtum** | wealth | masculine exception |
| der **Irrtum** | error, mistake | masculine exception |

> **Trap (critical):** the plural and article test. *Ein **Bedürfnis**, viele Bedürfnisse* — neuter; *eine **Erkenntnis**, viele Erkenntnisse* — feminine. In a dictionary entry the gender is the decisive information; in a text the article before the noun tells you which one you are dealing with. *Die Erkenntnis gewinnt an Bedeutung* vs. *Das Verständnis wächst.*

### 3.4. Verbalstil → Nominalstil: 8 Pairs

The conversion of a verbal sentence into nominal style has one basic move: **the finite verb becomes a noun (its -ung or equivalent form), and its object becomes a genitive.** *Wir erklären das Problem* → *die Erklärung **des Problems***. Word order changes too: the noun phrase replaces the whole clause and usually rides on a preposition like *nach*, *bei*, *wegen*, or *trotz*.

**8 pairs:**

| Verbalstil | Nominalstil | English |
|------------|-------------|---------|
| Wir erklären die Methode. | Die **Erklärung** der Methode | the explanation of the method |
| Der Arzt untersucht den Patienten. | Die **Untersuchung** des Patienten | the examination of the patient |
| Die Firma entwickelt ein neues Produkt. | Die **Entwicklung** eines neuen Produkts | the development of a new product |
| Man diskutiert die Reform. | Die **Diskussion** der Reform | the discussion of the reform |
| Die Regierung beschließt das Gesetz. | Der **Beschluss** des Gesetzes | the passing of the law |
| Sie verbessert die Bedingungen. | Die **Verbesserung** der Bedingungen | the improvement of the conditions |
| Er begründet seine These. | Die **Begründung** seiner These | the justification of his thesis |
| Die Stadt plant den Umbau. | Die **Planung** des Umbaus | the planning of the renovation |

> **Note:** notice the genitive cases in the right column: definite objects take the weak genitive (*des Patienten*), indefinite objects take the strong genitive (*eines neuen Produkts*). The nominal phrase usually appears inside a prepositional frame: *Nach der **Untersuchung** des Patienten ...*, *Wegen der **Erklärung** der Methode ...*

### 3.5. Genitive Chains: 4 Examples of Increasing Complexity

The nominal style often stacks genitives on genitives — the famous *die Untersuchung der Ursachen des Problems*. Each additional genitive answers "of what?" about the noun before it. The chain is read from left to right, each *des/der* attaching the following noun as the possessor or object of the preceding one.

**4 examples of increasing complexity:**

1. die **Lösung** des Problems (the solution of the problem)
2. die **Lösung** des Problems der Stadt (the solution of the problem of the city)
3. die **Untersuchung** der Ursachen des Problems (the investigation of the causes of the problem)
4. die **Untersuchung** der Ursachen des Problems der städtischen Verkehrsplanung (the investigation of the causes of the problem of urban transport planning)

> **Note (critical):** chains longer than three genitives become hard to read, and German writers then switch to *von*: *die Untersuchung der Ursachen **von** Problemen*. In your own writing, keep chains at two or three links and use *von* for the rest. Reading a chain: unpack it from the right — *Verkehrsplanung* (planning) ← *der städtischen* (of the urban) ← *des Problems* (of the problem) ← *der Ursachen* (of the causes) ← *die Untersuchung* (the investigation).

### 3.6. Nominalstil → Verbalstil: 6 Pairs

Converting nominal style back into verbal style is the skill you need for reading and for informal writing. The noun becomes a clause; the genitive object becomes the object of a verb; prepositional connectors become subordinating conjunctions. The table shows the standard conversions — note the tense and voice shifts.

**6 pairs:**

| Nominalstil | Verbalstil | English |
|-------------|------------|---------|
| Nach der **Ankunft** des Zuges ... | **Nachdem** der Zug **angekommen war** ... | After the arrival of the train ... |
| Trotz der **Ablehnung** des Antrags ... | **Obwohl** der Antrag **abgelehnt wurde** ... | Despite the rejection of the application ... |
| Bei der **Prüfung** der Unterlagen ... | **Als** die Unterlagen **geprüft wurden** ... | When the documents were examined ... |
| Die **Veröffentlichung** des Berichts verzögerte sich. | Der Bericht **wurde** spät **veröffentlicht**. | The publication of the report was delayed. |
| Wegen der **Erhöhung** der Preise ... | **Weil** die Preise **erhöht wurden** ... | Because of the increase in prices ... |
| Die **Lösung** des Problems gelang. | Es gelang, das Problem **zu lösen**. | The solution of the problem succeeded. |

> **Note:** the conversion table is the mirror image of §3.4. The fixed word-pairings to memorize: **nach → nachdem**, **trotz → obwohl**, **bei → als/wenn**, **wegen → weil/da**, and any noun in -ung → the corresponding verb in the right tense.

### 3.7. Register Choice: 4 Context Examples

The decision between Nominalstil and Verbalstil is a register decision. In conversation, in personal letters, and in anything spoken, use Verbalstil — nominal constructions sound stiff and unnatural out loud. In academic texts, official correspondence, press releases, and formal reports, use Nominalstil — verbal constructions sound sloppy and informal there.

**4 context examples:**

1. **Spoken (friend):** Ich denke, dass die Preise steigen. (I think prices are rising.) — **Written (report):** Die Preisentwicklung weist eine steigende Tendenz auf. (Price development shows a rising tendency.)
2. **Spoken (colleague):** Wir haben geprüft, ob das möglich ist. (We checked whether that is possible.) — **Written (memo):** Die Prüfung der Machbarkeit ergab positive Befunde. (The feasibility study produced positive findings.)
3. **Spoken (interview):** Wir haben die Ursache untersucht. (We investigated the cause.) — **Written (press release):** Die Untersuchung der Ursachen des Problems wurde abgeschlossen. (The investigation of the causes of the problem has been completed.)
4. **Spoken (public debate):** Weil die Regierung das Gesetz beschlossen hat, ändert sich die Lage. (Because the government has passed the law, the situation changes.) — **Written (commentary):** Mit dem Beschluss des Gesetzes hat sich die Rechtslage grundlegend geändert. (With the passing of the law, the legal situation has fundamentally changed.)

> **Rule of thumb:** if the text will be spoken, use Verbalstil; if it will be read, use Nominalstil. The middle ground — a light nominal style with short sentences — is what good journalistic writing looks like: not every clause nominalized, but the key processes expressed as nouns.
