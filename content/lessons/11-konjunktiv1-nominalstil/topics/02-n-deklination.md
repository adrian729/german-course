You learned the N-Deklination (also called the weak noun declension) in Lesson 4. This section is the full review you need at B2: the complete tables in singular and plural, the three irregular nouns **Herr, Name, Herz**, twenty high-frequency N-nouns grouped by their endings, and a four-rule decision procedure that lets you recognize an N-noun on sight. The N-Deklination matters now because newspapers and official texts are full of these nouns — and because the errors are among the most visible a B2 learner can make.

### 2.1. Declension Tables: Singular & Plural

An N-noun is a **masculine** noun that takes the ending **-(e)n in every case except the nominative singular**. In the plural the noun itself carries the ending -en, and the article does the work of showing the case. There are no umlaut plurals in the N-Deklination: *der Junge → die Jungen*, not ~~die Jünge~~.

**Table — der Junge (a regular N-noun):**

| Case | Singular | Plural |
|------|----------|--------|
| Nominativ | der **Junge** | die **Jungen** |
| Akkusativ | den **Jungen** | die **Jungen** |
| Dativ | dem **Jungen** | den **Jungen** |
| Genitiv | des **Jungen** | der **Jungen** |

**Table — der Student (an N-noun ending in -ent):**

| Case | Singular | Plural |
|------|----------|--------|
| Nominativ | der **Student** | die **Studenten** |
| Akkusativ | den **Studenten** | die **Studenten** |
| Dativ | dem **Studenten** | den **Studenten** |
| Genitiv | des **Studenten** | der **Studenten** |

> **Note (critical):** every non-nominative case adds **-en** (or just **-n** if the noun already ends in -e). There is no genitive -s anywhere in the regular N-Deklination: *des Student**en***, never ~~des Students~~ and never ~~des Studentens~~. And the plural of an N-noun always ends in -en or -n.

### 2.2. The Irregular Group: Herr, Name, Herz

Three very common nouns do not follow the regular pattern — they are the only real irregularities in the N-Deklination and they must be memorized. Note that **Herr** takes only **-n** in the singular (den Herrn), **Name** takes **-ns** in the genitive (des Namens), and **Herz** is the only neuter N-noun, with an **-ens** genitive and an **-en** dative.

**Table — Herr:**

| Case | Singular | Plural |
|------|----------|--------|
| Nominativ | der **Herr** | die **Herren** |
| Akkusativ | den **Herrn** | die **Herren** |
| Dativ | dem **Herrn** | den **Herren** |
| Genitiv | des **Herrn** | der **Herren** |

**Table — Name:**

| Case | Singular | Plural |
|------|----------|--------|
| Nominativ | der **Name** | die **Namen** |
| Akkusativ | den **Namen** | die **Namen** |
| Dativ | dem **Namen** | den **Namen** |
| Genitiv | des **Namens** | der **Namen** |

**Table — Herz:**

| Case | Singular | Plural |
|------|----------|--------|
| Nominativ | das **Herz** | die **Herzen** |
| Akkusativ | das **Herz** | die **Herzen** |
| Dativ | dem **Herzen** | den **Herzen** |
| Genitiv | des **Herzens** | der **Herzen** |

**The three irregular forms you must never get wrong:**

1. **den Herrn** — Akkusativ/Dativ/Genitiv singular of Herr: *Ich habe den **Herrn** gestern getroffen.*
2. **des Namens** — Genitiv singular of Name: *Die Bedeutung des **Namens** ist unbekannt.*
3. **des Herzens / dem Herzen** — Genitiv/Dativ of Herz: *Die Gesundheit des **Herzens** liegt mir am Herzen.*

> **Trap (critical):** the same irregular family contains **der Gedanke** (des Gedankens, den Gedanken) and **der Buchstabe** (des Buchstabens, den Buchstaben), which decline exactly like **Name**. And never put an -n on the nominative of Herr: *der **Herr***, not ~~der Herrn~~.

### 2.3. 20 Common N-Nouns by Ending Pattern

The N-Deklination is not a random list — almost every N-noun falls into one of a few recognizable ending patterns. Learn the patterns and you will recognize new N-nouns before you look them up. Here are twenty high-frequency examples grouped by pattern.

**Group 1 — nouns ending in -e that refer to a person or animal (6):**

| German | English | Notes |
|--------|---------|-------|
| der Junge (Jungen) | boy | -e → -en everywhere except nominative |
| der Kollege (Kollegen) | colleague (male) | -e → -en |
| der Kunde (Kunden) | customer, client | -e → -en |
| der Affe (Affen) | monkey | -e → -en |
| der Löwe (Löwen) | lion | -e → -en |
| der Franzose (Franzosen) | Frenchman | -e → -en |

**Group 2 — loanwords ending in -ent / -ant (3):**

| German | English | Notes |
|--------|---------|-------|
| der Student (Studenten) | student | stress on the final syllable |
| der Präsident (Präsidenten) | president | stress on the final syllable |
| der Assistent (Assistenten) | assistant | stress on the final syllable |

**Group 3 — loanwords ending in -ist (2):**

| German | English | Notes |
|--------|---------|-------|
| der Journalist (Journalisten) | journalist | stress on the final syllable |
| der Polizist (Polizisten) | police officer | stress on the final syllable |

**Group 4 — loanwords ending in -at / -oge / -graf (4):**

| German | English | Notes |
|--------|---------|-------|
| der Soldat (Soldaten) | soldier | -at |
| der Kandidat (Kandidaten) | candidate | -at |
| der Biologe (Biologen) | biologist | -oge |
| der Fotograf (Fotografen) | photographer | -graf |

**Group 5 — irregulars (5):**

| German | English | Notes |
|--------|---------|-------|
| der Mensch (Menschen) | human being | the most common N-noun of all |
| der Herr (Herren) | gentleman, Mr | only -n in the singular |
| der Name (Namen) | name | genitive -ns |
| der Nachbar (Nachbarn) | neighbour | ends in a vowel sound, takes -n |
| das Herz (Herzen) | heart | the only neuter N-noun |

> **Note:** the loanword groups (2–4) are all **international words with stress on the final syllable**. That is the single best recognition cue: if a masculine loanword ends in -ent, -ant, -ist, -at, -oge, -graf, or -ekt (der Architekt), it is an N-noun. The -e group (1) is the native German core, and group 5 is the memorization list.

### 2.4. Recognition Summary: 4 Decision Rules

When you meet a new masculine noun, run it through these four rules in order. The first rule that matches decides.

**Rule 1 — the -e test:** does the noun end in -e? If it is masculine and refers to a person or animal, it is an N-noun. Exceptions that behave like it even though they name things: der Name, der Gedanke, der Buchstabe. → *der Junge, der Kunde, der Kollege, der Affe.*

**Rule 2 — the loanword test:** is it a masculine loanword with a stressed final syllable ending in -ent, -ant, -ist, -at, -oge, -graf, or -ekt? Then it is an N-noun. → *der Student, der Präsident, der Journalist, der Soldat, der Biologe, der Fotograf, der Architekt.*

**Rule 3 — the irregular list:** does it belong to the short memorized list der Mensch, der Herr, der Name, der Nachbar, das Herz (plus der Gedanke, der Buchstabe)? Then decline it as an N-noun, with the special forms of §2.2. → *der Mensch, der Nachbar, das Herz.*

**Rule 4 — everything else:** feminine nouns, neuter nouns other than Herz, and masculine nouns ending in -er, -el, -en, -ling, -ig, or -ich are **not** N-nouns; they decline normally. → *der Lehrer (Lehrers), der Wagen (Wagens), das Mädchen (Mädchens), die Frau (Frau).*

**4 decision-rule examples:**

1. **der Kunde** — ends in -e, male person → Rule 1: den Kunden, dem Kunden, des Kunden.
2. **der Politiker** — ends in -er → Rule 4: normal declension, des Politikers (not an N-noun).
3. **der Biologe** — ends in -oge, stressed final syllable → Rule 2: den Biologen, dem Biologen, des Biologen.
4. **das Kind** — neuter, not Herz → Rule 4: normal declension, des Kindes.

> **Trap (critical):** the most frequent learner error is treating a Rule-4 noun like an N-noun (*das Geschenk des Politikers* is correct as written; the error would be ~~des Politiken~~) or missing a Rule-1 noun (*das Buch des Kunde**n***, not ~~des Kundes~~). Run the four rules every time and the ending will be right.
