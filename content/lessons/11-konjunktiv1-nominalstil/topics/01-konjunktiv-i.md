In Lesson 9 you met the Konjunktiv I (K1) as the signal of **indirect speech**: the mood that a reporter uses to say "this is what someone said, without me vouching for it". *Der Minister sagt, die Preise **seien** stabil.* This week you complete the system: the full conjugation of the five verbs that matter most, the Perfekt and Futur forms, the substitution rule that sends ambiguous forms into the Konjunktiv II, and the special case of commands in indirect speech. By the end of this section you will be able to read and write a complete newspaper paragraph entirely in reported speech.

### 1.1. Conjugation Tables: sein, haben, werden, können, gehen

K1 is built by taking the verb stem and adding the endings **-e, -est, -e, -en, -et, -en** — with the small complication that the stem itself often changes (sein, haben, the modals, and werden use their special K1 stems, which you learned in Lesson 9). The conjugation of every verb follows one of only two patterns: either it uses the special K1 stem, or it uses the ordinary Präsens stem.

**Table 1 — sein (the anchor verb of the whole system):**

| Person | Konjunktiv I | English | Identical to indicative? |
|--------|--------------|---------|--------------------------|
| ich | **sei** | be | no |
| du | **seiest** | be | no |
| er/sie/es | **sei** | be | no |
| wir | **seien** | be | no |
| ihr | **seiet** | be | no |
| sie | **seien** | be | no |

**Table 2 — haben:**

| Person | Konjunktiv I | English | Identical to indicative? |
|--------|--------------|---------|--------------------------|
| ich | **habe** | have | **yes** (indicative: ich habe) |
| du | **habest** | have | no (indicative: du hast) |
| er/sie/es | **habe** | have | no (indicative: er hat) |
| wir | **haben** | have | **yes** (indicative: wir haben) |
| ihr | **habet** | have | no (indicative: ihr habt) |
| sie | **haben** | have | **yes** (indicative: sie haben) |

**Table 3 — werden:**

| Person | Konjunktiv I | English | Identical to indicative? |
|--------|--------------|---------|--------------------------|
| ich | **werde** | will / become | **yes** (indicative: ich werde) |
| du | **werdest** | will / become | no (indicative: du wirst) |
| er/sie/es | **werde** | will / become | no (indicative: er wird) |
| wir | **werden** | will / become | **yes** (indicative: wir werden) |
| ihr | **werdet** | will / become | **yes** (indicative: ihr werdet) |
| sie | **werden** | will / become | **yes** (indicative: sie werden) |

**Table 4 — können:**

| Person | Konjunktiv I | English | Identical to indicative? |
|--------|--------------|---------|--------------------------|
| ich | **könne** | can | no (indicative: ich kann) |
| du | **könnest** | can | no (indicative: du kannst) |
| er/sie/es | **könne** | can | no (indicative: er kann) |
| wir | **können** | can | **yes** (indicative: wir können) |
| ihr | **könnet** | can | no (indicative: ihr könnt) |
| sie | **können** | can | **yes** (indicative: sie können) |

**Table 5 — gehen (a regular weak verb):**

| Person | Konjunktiv I | English | Identical to indicative? |
|--------|--------------|---------|--------------------------|
| ich | **gehe** | go | **yes** (indicative: ich gehe) |
| du | **gehest** | go | no (indicative: du gehst) |
| er/sie/es | **gehe** | go | no (indicative: er geht) |
| wir | **gehen** | go | **yes** (indicative: wir gehen) |
| ihr | **gehet** | go | no (indicative: ihr geht) |
| sie | **gehen** | go | **yes** (indicative: sie gehen) |

> **Note (critical):** study the "Identical to indicative?" column. For sein, every form is distinct, so K1 can always be used. For haben, werden, können, and every ordinary verb, the **1st person singular and the 1st/3rd person plural are identical to the indicative**. That is precisely the ambiguity that forces the substitution rule of §1.4: *sie **haben** gekauft* in a report could mean "they bought" (fact) or "they are said to have bought" (report) — so the reporter switches to the K2 substitute *sie **hätten** gekauft*.

### 1.2. Konjunktiv I Perfekt: er sei gekommen

For events that happened **before** the time of speaking, German uses **K1 of the auxiliary (haben or sein) + Partizip II**. The auxiliary is the one from the Perfekt (Lesson 2): movement and change of state take sein, everything else takes haben. The K1 forms of the auxiliary are: **habe/habest/habe/haben/habet/haben** and **sei/seiest/sei/seien/seiet/seien**.

Formation: **sei / habe (conjugated in K1) + Partizip II at the end.**

**6 examples:**

1. Er **sei** gestern **gekommen**. (He came yesterday. — sein-verb)
2. Sie **habe** das **gesagt**. (She said that. — haben-verb)
3. Der Minister **habe** seinen Rücktritt **erklärt**. (The minister announced his resignation.)
4. Die Regierung **sei** zurückgetreten. (The government resigned. — sein-verb)
5. Der Zeuge **habe** vor Gericht **gelogen**. (The witness lied in court.)
6. Die Preise **seien** im letzten Jahr stark **gestiegen**. (Prices rose sharply last year. — sein-verb)

> **Trap:** K1 Perfekt has nothing to do with reality — it simply marks the reported event as earlier than the moment of reporting. *Er sei gekommen* does not claim that he came, only that someone says he came. If you are sure of the fact, you stay in the indicative: *Er ist gestern gekommen.*

### 1.3. Konjunktiv I Futur: er werde kommen

For events that will happen **after** the time of speaking, German uses **K1 of werden + Infinitiv at the end**. Formation: **werde/werdest/werde/werden/werdet/werden + Infinitiv.**

**4 examples:**

1. Er **werde** morgen **kommen**. (He will come tomorrow.)
2. Die Firma **werde** im Ausland expandieren. (The company will expand abroad.)
3. Der Zug **werde** pünktlich **ankommen**. (The train will arrive on time.)
4. Der Präsident **werde** sich dazu äußern. (The president will comment on this.)

> **Note:** the K1 Futur is the reported-speech version of the Futur I (Lesson 6). In everyday informal reports Germans often substitute the indicative or *würde* (K2), but in written news the K1 Futur is the standard form — you will see *werde* constantly in newspaper articles.

### 1.4. Substitution: Konjunktiv I → Konjunktiv II

Whenever a K1 form is **identical to the indicative**, the report no longer signals "this is what someone said" — the reader cannot tell the K1 from an ordinary statement of fact. German solves this with the **Ersatzform** (substitute form): the corresponding **Konjunktiv II**. The substitution happens most often in the 1st person singular and the 1st/3rd person plural of regular verbs, and in **haben** and **werden**.

**The substitution chart:**

| Verb | K1 (3rd person plural) | Identical to indicative? | K2 substitute used in reports |
|------|------------------------|--------------------------|-------------------------------|
| haben | sie **haben** | yes | sie **hätten** |
| sein | sie **seien** | no | — (only *sie wären* if doubt is expressed) |
| gehen | sie **gehen** | yes | sie **würden gehen** |
| kommen | sie **kommen** | yes | sie **kämen** |
| sagen | sie **sagen** | yes | sie **würden sagen** |
| gesagt haben | sie **haben gesagt** | yes | sie **hätten gesagt** |
| sein (Präteritum content) | sie **seien gewesen** | no | — |

**How to read the chart:** for haben, the 3rd person plural K1 *sie haben* is indistinguishable from the indicative, so the newspaper writes *Die Politiker sagen, sie **hätten** keine Zeit.* For sein no substitution is normally needed — *sie seien* is unmistakably K1. For regular verbs the substitute is **würde + Infinitiv** (*sie würden gehen*), and for strong verbs the synthetic K2 (*sie kämen*) is preferred in elegant written style.

**3 examples showing the substitution at work:**

1. Die Nachbarn sagen, sie **hätten** das Haus verkauft. (The neighbours say they sold the house. — K2 substitute for *sie haben verkauft*)
2. Die Demonstranten sagen, sie **würden** weiter demonstrieren. (The demonstrators say they will keep demonstrating. — K2 substitute for *sie demonstrieren*)
3. Die Kollegen sagen, sie **kämen** zu spät. (The colleagues say they will come late. — K2 substitute for *sie kommen*)

> **Note (critical):** the substitution is a **form replacement, not a meaning change**. *Sie hätten das Haus verkauft* still reports the neighbours' statement; it does not make it unreal. The K2 form is used here only because the K1 form looks exactly like the indicative.

### 1.5. Model Newspaper Paragraph

The paragraph below is written the way a German newspaper reports a press conference. Read it and notice how the tense of the K1 follows the tense of the original statement: present → **K1 Präsens**, past → **K1 Perfekt**, future → **K1 Futur**, ambiguous plural forms → **K2 substitutes**. The introductory verb in the indicative (*sagte*, *erklärte*) is the signal that everything that follows is reported speech. There are **no quotation marks** anywhere.

**Model paragraph (8 sentences):**

1. Der Ministerpräsident **habe** bei der Pressekonferenz **erklärt**, die Sanierung des Bahnhofs **beginne** im Frühjahr. (The prime minister explained at the press conference that the renovation of the station would begin in spring.)
2. Die Bauarbeiten **würden** etwa zwei Jahre **dauern**. (The construction work would last about two years. — K2 substitute)
3. Bereits im vergangenen Monat **sei** ein Gutachten **vorgelegt worden**. (A report had already been submitted last month. — K1 Perfekt, passiv)
4. Die Opposition **habe** Zweifel an der Finanzierung **angemeldet**. (The opposition has voiced doubts about the financing. — K1 Perfekt)
5. Der Koalitionspartner **werde** dem Vorhaben jedoch **zustimmen**. (The coalition partner, however, will approve the project. — K1 Futur)
6. Nach Angaben des Finanzministeriums **seien** die Mittel bereits **eingeplant**. (According to the finance ministry, the funds have already been budgeted. — K1 Perfekt)
7. Ein Sprecher **sagte**, die Terminplanung **hätte** nicht geändert werden **müssen**. (A spokesman said the schedule would not have had to be changed. — K2 substitute)
8. Man **werde** die Bürger rechtzeitig **informieren**. (The public will be informed in good time. — K1 Futur)

> **Note:** sentence 7 is the classic substitution case — *die Terminplanung **hätte** nicht geändert werden **müssen**.* The K1 of the modal construction would be ambiguous, so the reporter uses the K2. In sentence 4 the K1 Perfekt of the 3rd person singular is unambiguous, so the pure K1 is kept.

### 1.6. Imperative in Indirect Speech

Commands, requests, and instructions in indirect speech never keep the imperative form. There are **two standard constructions**: the report uses **sollen + Infinitiv** (by far the most common), and the formal written style uses the **K1 imperative** — a fixed, archaic-looking K1 form of the verb in the 3rd person, typical of recipes, instructions, and official notices.

**3 examples:**

1. **Er sagte, sie solle sofort kommen.** (He told her to come at once. — *sollen* + Infinitiv)
2. **Man nehme 200 g Mehl und rühre es unter den Teig.** (Take 200 g of flour and stir it into the batter. — formal K1 imperative, recipe style)
3. **Der Lehrer sagte, die Schüler sollten leiser sein.** (The teacher said the pupils should be quieter. — *sollen* in K2 substitute)

> **Note:** the *man nehme* style is a genuine K1 form used as a written instruction without any reporting verb — you meet it in recipes (*Man nehme...*), instructions, and legal texts. The spoken alternative is *Nehmen Sie 200 g Mehl* (ordinary imperative, Lesson 5); the *man nehme* form belongs to writing.
