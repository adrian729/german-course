### 5.1. Temporal Conjunctions

These eight subordinating conjunctions express **time**. All of them send the verb to the end of the clause.

| Conjunction | Meaning | Use | Example |
|-------------|---------|-----|---------|
| als | when | one single event in the past | **Als** ich jung war, wohnte ich in Hamburg. (When I was young, I lived in Hamburg.) |
| wenn | when, whenever | repeated events or future events | **Wenn** ich Zeit habe, lese ich ein Buch. (When I have time, I read a book.) |
| nachdem | after | one event after another | **Nachdem** ich gegessen habe, mache ich einen Spaziergang. (After I have eaten, I go for a walk.) |
| bevor | before | one event before another | **Bevor** wir gehen, räumen wir auf. (Before we leave, we tidy up.) |
| während | while | two events at the same time | **Während** sie kocht, hört sie Musik. (While she cooks, she listens to music.) |
| seitdem | since | from a point in the past until now | **Seitdem** er in Berlin wohnt, spricht er besser Deutsch. (Since he lives in Berlin, he speaks German better.) |
| bis | until | up to a point in time | Warte, **bis** der Regen aufhört. (Wait until the rain stops.) |
| sobald | as soon as | immediately after | **Sobald** ich ankomme, rufe ich dich an. (As soon as I arrive, I will call you.) |

> **als vs. wenn — the critical distinction:** use **als** for a **single, completed event in the past** (Als ich gestern ankam, ...). Use **wenn** for **repeated events** (Wenn es regnet, ...) and for **future or general** time (Wenn ich Zeit habe, ...). A sentence like ~~Als ich Zeit habe~~ is wrong — future/repeated requires wenn.
>
> **nachdem:** the A2 form pairs nachdem + Perfekt with a main clause in Präsens (Nachdem ich gegessen habe, sehe ich fern). The full rule — nachdem + Plusquamperfekt, main clause in Präteritum — comes in Lesson 7.

### 5.2. Causal & Conditional Conjunctions: da, falls

**da** (since, as) introduces a **reason** the speaker treats as already known — it typically stands in position 1, with the main clause following.

1. **Da** es regnet, bleiben wir zu Hause. (Since it is raining, we are staying home.)
2. **Da** ich müde bin, gehe ich früh ins Bett. (Since I am tired, I am going to bed early.)

**falls** (in case, if) introduces a **condition** — less certain than wenn, often with a request or plan in the main clause.

3. **Falls** du Zeit hast, besuch uns. (In case you have time, visit us.)
4. **Falls** es regnet, nehmen wir den Bus. (In case it rains, we will take the bus.)

> **weil vs. da:** both mean "because/since" and both are verb-final. **weil** gives a new reason the listener may not know (often in second position in the sentence); **da** gives a reason already established (usually sentence-initial): *Ich bleibe zu Hause, weil es regnet.* vs. *Da es regnet, bleibe ich zu Hause.* (Lesson 2 introduced weil; da and falls are new here.)

### 5.3. Verb-Final Rule Summary Chart

All **subordinating conjunctions** — the ones you have met in Lesson 2 (weil, dass, wenn, obwohl) and all the new ones from this week — send the **conjugated verb to the end** of their clause.

| Conjunction | Meaning | Clause Type | Example |
|-------------|---------|-------------|---------|
| dass | that | content clause | Ich weiß, dass er kommt. |
| ob | whether | indirect question | Ich weiß nicht, ob er kommt. |
| weil | because | causal | Ich bleibe zu Hause, weil es regnet. |
| da | since, as | causal (known reason) | Da es regnet, bleibe ich zu Hause. |
| falls | in case | conditional | Falls es regnet, nehme ich den Bus. |
| wenn | when, if | temporal / conditional | Wenn ich Zeit habe, lese ich. |
| als | when (single past) | temporal | Als er ankam, rief er an. |
| nachdem | after | temporal | Nachdem ich gegessen habe, sehe ich fern. |
| bevor | before | temporal | Bevor wir gehen, räumen wir auf. |
| während | while | temporal | Während sie kocht, hört sie Musik. |
| seitdem | since | temporal | Seitdem er hier wohnt, lerne ich ihn kennen. |
| bis | until | temporal | Warte, bis der Regen aufhört. |
| sobald | as soon as | temporal | Sobald ich ankomme, rufe ich an. |
| obwohl | although | concessive | Obwohl er müde war, ging er zur Arbeit. |

> **Contrast with coordinating conjunctions (Lesson 2, §8):** und, aber, oder, denn, sondern do **not** send the verb to the end — the clause keeps normal V2 order. Subordinating conjunctions always pull the verb to the end. One verb, two word-order systems.

### 5.4. Main Clause After Subordinate Clause: Verb First

When a subordinate clause stands **first**, the main clause must start with the **verb** (the V2 rule, Lesson 1: one element in position 1, verb in position 2). The whole subordinate clause counts as that single element.

1. **Wenn** es regnet, **bleiben** wir zu Hause. (If it rains, we stay home.)
2. **Weil** ich müde bin, **gehe** ich früh ins Bett. (Because I am tired, I go to bed early.)
3. **Nachdem** sie gegessen hat, **sieht** sie fern. (After she has eaten, she watches TV.)
4. **Falls** du Zeit hast, **besuch** uns. (In case you have time, visit us.)

> **Note:** do not put the subject before the verb in the main clause after a subordinate clause: ~~Wenn es regnet, wir bleiben zu Hause.~~ is wrong. The verb comes first, then the subject.

### 5.5. Coordinating Adverbs: Position 1 + Inversion

**Coordinating adverbs** (also called conjunctive adverbs) link two main clauses. They do **not** send the verb to the end, but they **trigger inversion**: the adverb counts as position 1, so the verb jumps to position 2 — right after the adverb, before the subject.

| Adverb | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| deshalb | therefore, that's why |
| deswegen | therefore, because of that |
| darum | therefore, that's why |
| trotzdem | nevertheless, still |
| außerdem | besides, in addition |
| also | so, therefore |

**6 examples (adverb in position 1 + inversion):**

1. Es regnet, **deshalb** bleibe ich zu Hause. (It is raining, therefore I am staying home.)
2. Er ist krank, **trotzdem** geht er zur Arbeit. (He is sick, nevertheless he goes to work.)
3. Sie hat viel gearbeitet, **deswegen** ist sie müde. (She worked a lot, therefore she is tired.)
4. Ich habe keine Zeit, **darum** kann ich nicht kommen. (I have no time, that's why I cannot come.)
5. Sie spricht gut Deutsch, **außerdem** lernt sie Spanisch. (She speaks German well, besides she is learning Spanish.)
6. Er hat keine Lust, **also** geht er nicht mit. (He doesn't feel like it, so he is not coming along.)

The same adverbs can also start the whole sentence — with the same inversion:

- **Deshalb** bleibe ich zu Hause. (That's why I am staying home.)
- **Trotzdem** geht er zur Arbeit. (Nevertheless he goes to work.)

> **Contrast:** coordinating adverbs behave like a time phrase or object in position 1 (inversion, verb stays in position 2) — they are **not** subordinating conjunctions and never push the verb to the end. Compare: *..., weil ich müde bin.* (verb-final) vs. *..., deshalb bin ich müde.* (inversion).

### 5.6. Position of nicht in Complex Sentences

Five rules cover the position of **nicht** (Lesson 1, §8) in longer sentences:

**Rule 1 — Negating the whole predicate in a simple main clause: nicht goes to the very end** of the sentence, after all complements and adverbs.

- Ich schlafe **nicht**. (I am not sleeping.)
- Er kommt heute **nicht**. (He is not coming today.)

**Rule 2 — With end-of-sentence elements: nicht goes before the Partizip II or the separated prefix.**

- Ich bin **nicht** gekommen. (I did not come.)
- Ich stehe **nicht** auf. (I am not getting up.)
- Wir haben **nicht** eingekauft. (We did not shop.)

**Rule 3 — Negating a specific element: nicht goes directly before that element.**

- Ich trinke **nicht** Kaffee, sondern Tee. (I don't drink coffee, but tea.)
- Das Buch ist **nicht** neu, sondern alt. (The book is not new, but old.)

**Rule 4 — In subordinate clauses: nicht goes before the conjugated verb at the end** of the clause.

- ..., weil ich heute **nicht** komme. (...because I am not coming today.)
- ..., obwohl sie das Buch **nicht** versteht. (...although she doesn't understand the book.)

**Rule 5 — With modal verbs + infinitive: nicht goes before the infinitive.**

- Ich kann heute **nicht** kommen. (I cannot come today.)
- Sie will **nicht** mitgehen. (She doesn't want to come along.)

> **Quick check:** find the "end block" of the sentence — the part that carries the tense (infinitive, participle, separated prefix, or the conjugated verb at the end of a subordinate clause). nicht sits directly before that block when it negates the whole sentence, or directly before the specific element it negates (Rule 3).
